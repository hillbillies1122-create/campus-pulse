Data sources and method

Campus Pulse uses published, citable data. This page lists every source and explains exactly how each number on the site is calculated, so you can check it or reuse it in a report.

1. Career data: O*NET

All 32 careers come from O*NET OnLine, the occupational database of the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration. For each career we use its O*NET-SOC code and these O*NET fields:

  • Interests: the six Occupational Interest scores (Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional), 0–100.
  • Skills: importance (0–100) of 12 skills: Programming, Mathematics, Science, Critical Thinking, Complex Problem Solving, Technology Design, Troubleshooting, Systems Analysis, Quality Control Analysis, Writing, Speaking, Coordination.
  • Knowledge: importance (0–100) of 10 areas: Engineering and Technology, Computers and Electronics, Physics, Design, Mechanical, Chemistry, Biology, Building and Construction, Telecommunications, Production and Processing.
  • Description, sample job titles, key tasks (paraphrased), job zone and example tools.

Data retrieved from O*NET OnLine on 21 October 2026. Each career page links to its O*NET source page. O*NET content is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Several Campus Pulse career names add Indian context, for example "Computer Hardware / Embedded Engineer" is O*NET 17-2061.00 Computer Hardware Engineers; O*NET has no separate "embedded" or "VLSI" occupation.

Limitation: O*NET describes jobs in the United States. What the work involves transfers well to India, but pay, demand and hiring practices do not, so Campus Pulse does not show U.S. salary or outlook figures.

2. Interest questions: O*NET Interest Profiler

The 60 activities come from the O*NET Interest Profiler Short Form (U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration), used under the O*NET Tools Developer License. Campus Pulse modified it as follows: items are interleaved across the six areas, each item is answered Like / Not sure / Dislike, and results are shown on screen.

Scoring. Each area has 10 items. Like = 1 point, Not sure = 0.5, Dislike = 0. Area score = points ÷ 10 × 100. Your interest code is the three highest areas, in order.

3. Matching method

Interest fit (0–100)

The Pearson correlation r between your six interest scores and the career's six O*NET interest scores, multiplied by 100. A negative correlation (opposite shape) counts as 0. It measures whether the shape of your profile matches the career's (which areas are high and low), not the absolute level.

Skill readiness (0–100)

O*NET rates importance on a 1–5 scale and publishes it as 0–100 using (I − 1) ÷ 4 × 100. Campus Pulse converts it back: expected level = 1 + importance ÷ 25. Only skills and knowledge areas with importance of 50 or more (rated "important" or higher) are used. For each one, we take min(your rating ÷ expected level, 1), then average these and multiply by 100.

Overall match

Match = 0.6 × interest fit + 0.4 × readiness. Interests get more weight because skills can be learned, while interests tend to be more stable. If you have only done one part, the match uses that part alone. These weights are a design choice by Campus Pulse, not an O*NET rule.

Marks and CGPA are not used in matching. They are shown back to you for reflection only.

4. India context: India Skills Report 2026

Employability rates by branch come from the India Skills Report 2026, released by ETS with CII, AICTE, AIU and Taggd, based on more than 100,000 candidates who took the Global Employability Test. Figures used: Computer Science 80%, Information Technology 78%, Instrumentation 77%, Electronics and Communication 75%, Mechanical 63%, all B.E./B.Tech 70.15%, national 56.35%. Branches without a published figure are not shown.

Reported by The CSR Journal and Careers360, November 2025. Note that "employability" here is a test-based readiness score, not a job-placement or unemployment rate.

5. Campus Pulse's own statistics

The Insights page shows averages across everyone using Campus Pulse: average interest scores, averages by branch, and average skill ratings. These are calculated live in the database. A group is only shown once it has at least 5 people, so nobody can be identified. No individual's answers are ever shown to anyone else.

6. Roadmap steps

The four starter steps on each career are suggestions written by Campus Pulse. They are not from O*NET. Edit or replace them freely.
